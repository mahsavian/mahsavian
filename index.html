From BBS to Blackboard: The Pedagogy of the Exploit

STS Corpus Linguistics Voyant Tools

This research evaluates the evolution of technical knowledge transfer through a comprehensive analysis of Phrack Magazine, a cornerstone of unauthorized technical documentation. By applying sociocultural learning theories to the domain of systems security, this thesis explores how technical identity and exploit methodologies are negotiated within decentralized networks. The core of the research employs a data-driven approach, integrating Voyant Tools for corpus linguistics to analyze decades of publication data. The analysis demonstrates that Phrack operates as a sophisticated, albeit informal, educational system that exerts significant influence over the "security mindset." The results highlight a paradox: while the magazine champions anti-authoritarian decentralization, its technical discourse maintains a rigid, culturally exclusive boundary. This work contributes to the field of digital technical education by defining hacker rhetoric as a structured, high-stakes instructional system that governs the dissemination of specialized security intelligence.

Geospatial Analysis: Non-EU Residences in Italy

ArcGIS Big Data Visualization Demographics

Developed an interactive ArcGIS dashboard to visualize the distribution and legal status of non-EU residents across Italian regions. This project utilized proportional symbol mapping and big data visualization to analyze demographic trends in Lombardy and other key administrative areas in 2021.

Shapes of Educational Gender Discrimination in Iran

Critical Discourse Analysis Gender Studies

An investigation into institutionalized gender bias within the Iranian educational system[cite: 1, 11]. The research focuses on university admission quotas, where at least 30% of seats are often reserved for male students [cite: 104], and the reinforcement of patriarchal attitudes in textbooks, where male representation exceeds female representation nearly three to one[cite: 142, 144]. The study also maps legal barriers preventing women from entering specific professional fields, such as the judiciary or mining[cite: 169].

Grave Injustice: NAGPRA and Repatriation Ethics

Institutional Ethics Indigenous Rights

A comprehensive analysis of the Native American Graves Protection and Repatriation Act (NAGPRA)[cite: 254]. The study explores the intersection of scientific authority and Indigenous sovereignty through high-profile case studies, such as the Kennewick Man[cite: 257, 308]. It examines the legal process of establishing cultural affiliation through geographical, biological, and oral tradition evidence [cite: 292], highlighting the role of repatriation departments in fostering institutional respect[cite: 323, 337].
