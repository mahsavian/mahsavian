The Narrative

I am a researcher and educator bridging 12 years of pedagogical practice with a scholarly focus on the "underground" origins of our digital world. My research is defined by a singular question: How does expertise survive the transition from informal, radical spaces to formal institutional structures?

My work is anchored in my Master’s research at the University of Padua, where I conducted a socio-technical autopsy of the pre-web digital underground. My thesis, "From BBS to Blackboard," utilized digital discourse analysis to map how early hacker forums functioned as alternative university systems for a generation of innovators.

Key Research & Digital Works

From BBS to Blackboard: The Pedagogy of the Exploit

STS Corpus Linguistics Voyant Tools

This research evaluates the evolution of technical knowledge transfer through a comprehensive analysis of Phrack Magazine, a cornerstone of unauthorized technical documentation. By applying sociocultural learning theories to the domain of systems security, this thesis explores how technical identity and exploit methodologies are negotiated within decentralized networks. Utilizing Voyant Tools for corpus linguistics to analyze decades of publication data, the analysis highlights a paradox: while the magazine champions anti-authoritarian decentralization, its technical discourse maintains a rigid, culturally exclusive boundary. This work contributes to digital technical education by defining hacker rhetoric as a structured, high-stakes instructional system that governs the dissemination of specialized security intelligence.

Geospatial Analysis: Non-EU Residences in Italy

ArcGIS Big Data Visualization Demographics

Developed an interactive ArcGIS dashboard visualizing the distribution and legal status of non-EU residents in Italy for 2021. Utilizing proportional symbol mapping and data visualization across key administrative regions such as Lombardy and Veneto, the project facilitates a quantitative assessment of migration patterns and residency status.

Shapes of Educational Gender Discrimination in Iran

Gender Studies Policy Analysis STS

An investigation into institutionalized gender bias within the Iranian educational system[cite: 88]. This research maps multifaceted barriers, including university admission quotas that reserve at least 30% of seats for male students [cite: 104], and identifies significant gender representation gaps in textbooks—where male representation exceeds female nearly three to one[cite: 142]. The study also examines legal and social constraints on professional advancement, such as prohibitions against women serving as judges or in the military[cite: 169].

Grave Injustice: NAGPRA and Repatriation Ethics

Institutional Ethics Indigenous Rights

Analysis of the Native American Graves Protection and Repatriation Act (NAGPRA), focusing on the struggle for cultural preservation and institutional justice[cite: 254]. Through high-profile case studies like the Kennewick Man, the research explores the ethical tensions between scientific authority and Indigenous sovereignty[cite: 308]. It assesses the evolution of museum practices, particularly the role of repatriation departments in negotiating cultural affiliation based on biological and oral tradition evidence[cite: 323].
